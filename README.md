## Abstract

**Background.** Classical capital-circulation formulas such as C − M − C and M − C − M′ capture the core distinction between commodity exchange and capital as self-expanding value, but their usual scalar form is too limited for analyzing heterogeneous, nonlinear, and dynamic accumulation processes.

**Framework.** This paper develops a general formula for capital accumulation by modeling capital motion as  
K_{t+1} = K_t + A(K_t, x_t) + u_t,  
where K_t is a capital-state variable and x_t is a vector of accumulation-relevant features. The framework incorporates linear, quadratic, polynomial, nonparametric, constrained, dynamic, threshold, and regime-switching specifications.

**Extension.** The model is further extended to an infinite-dimensional Hilbert-space formulation, allowing distributed features and kernel-based interactions to enter the accumulation law.

**Empirical Illustration.** A computational implementation is conducted using quarterly United States macroeconomic data. Because the available data do not directly measure Marxian capital stock, real GDP is used as an aggregate capital-state proxy, and the empirical target is the one-period variation ΔK_t = K_{t+1} − K_t. A ridge-regularized finite-dimensional approximation achieves a training R² of 0.2373, a test MAE of 121.1827, and a test RMSE of 143.1279.

**Contribution.** The paper contributes a unified mathematical and econometric framework for capital accumulation. It preserves the classical theoretical intuition of capital as self-expanding value while generalizing it into a flexible model with multi-feature determinants, nonlinear coupling, dynamic feedback, constraints, thresholds, and infinite-dimensional interactions.
